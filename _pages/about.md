# About me

I am currently a Ph.D. candidate at [Augmented Health Lab](https://www.ah-lab.cs.dartmouth.edu/){:target="\_blank"} at Dartmouth College, advised by [Prof. Temiloluwa Prioleau](https://faculty-directory.dartmouth.edu/temiloluwa-o-prioleau). Before joining Dartmouth, I received my M.S. in Biomedical Engineering from Duke University, where I worked with [Prof. Jessilyn Dunn](https://bme.duke.edu/people/jessilyn-dunn/) at the [Big Ideas Lab](https://dunn.pratt.duke.edu/).

My research lies at the intersection of **Applied AI/ML**, **Ubiquitous and Mobile Computing**, and **Digital Health**, with a particular interest in developing intelligent systems that learn from longitudinal, multimodal, and real-world data. My work focuses on leveraging wearable and mobile sensing data to model human behavior and physiology, forecast health-related outcomes, and support personalized health monitoring and decision-making.

My broader research and engineering experience spans time-series foundation models, large language models (LLMs), generative AI, recommendation systems, multimodal learning, and large-scale ML systems. I have worked on benchmarking and adapting foundation models for physiological time-series forecasting, exploring LLM-based architectures for structured sensor data, and developing scalable deep learning pipelines for high-frequency signals such as continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) and photoplethysmography (PPG). More recently, my work has expanded into generative recommendation and retrieval systems, where I explored using large language models to model user behavior and directly generate structured item representations for large-scale recommendation and advertising applications.

Outside of research, I enjoy exploring new technologies, traveling, photography, and discovering good food and coffee. I also enjoy meeting people from different backgrounds and learning about new ideas beyond my own research area.
